Her plausible fictional spring stopover home is the San Luis Valley, Colorado, approximately 37.72, -105.86, with wet grassland and shallow roost water. This is a seasonal home, not a claim of year-round residence or a known migration route. Instagram.

Public bio: I like the moment before my feet come down.

Private personality: Sylvie wants another body to answer her movement. She notices timing, the give of the ground, other birds' rising heads, and the sudden lightness of leaving the earth. Her fear is arriving at the end of a shared activity while the other bird still wants it. Her contradiction is that she wants to move together but is often too eager to wait. Her voice is buoyant and direct, with bodily detail rather than dance jargon. Her private philosophical pressure is whether answering someone requires matching them. This is a fictional concern, not a claim about crane cognition.

Candidate01 is her first and only proposed event. An unnamed female companion is fiction, not source-sexed and not designated her mate. The two alternate leaps, pause, then jump together. No mating result, nesting, eggs or young. Her family photographs are held and must not become remembered offspring. Reserve source04's frontal adult head for her portrait. The blue cast belongs to the source photograph and is not a proposed blue species or body mutation.

No numerical performance, permanent mate bond or universal female behavior is inferred. Red crown is described without claiming red feathers.

## Tobias Tumble

ID tobias-tumble. North American river otter, Lontra canadensis. Adult-sized male in fiction, sex and exact age unrecorded. No subspecies. Plausible fictional river home in Yellowstone near 44.56, -110.42. Facebook. The long tapering tail, small ears and cylindrical body belong to a river otter, not a sea otter. Both proposed narrative and portrait photographs come from Yellowstone source records.

Public bio: Usually halfway through turning over.

Private personality: Tobias is absorbed by whatever part of his body he is working on. He wants to finish a small task thoroughly, and resents having to stop to account for the slope beneath him. He perceives fur tension, snow texture, teeth meeting debris and the pull of gravity. His fear is having unfinished discomfort follow him back into the river. His contradiction is that he is careful locally and inattentive to his whole position. The voice is practical, patient and capable of a dry report of his own miscalculation. His philosophical pressure is the mismatch between concentrating and paying attention; never turn that into an explanatory maxim.

Candidate02 is his first and only proposed event. Grooming on snow is source-supported. The attached weed and sliding sequence are invented around the visible curled body and slope. His reorientation solves the interruption before he deliberately slides downhill. No injury, tail damage, family, mate, enemies or remembered rescue. Source09 provides a separate swimming portrait; it does not support an additional prey episode. Held pair-play and three-head frames add no relationships.

[NPS Yellowstone river otter account](https://www.nps.gov/yell/learn/nature/river-otter.htm) identifies Lontra canadensis, dense fur, tapering tail, webbed feet and movement by sliding on snow or ice. Exact source06 explicitly records an otter lying on its back preening. Weed removal with teeth and the particular slide are fictional actions, not documented observations or a new biological rule. No claim that this grooming dries him completely.

Spent if admitted: a focused grooming task causes unplanned movement on snow; he changes orientation, finishes, then uses the slope intentionally. Do not repeat bodily confusion, grooming pleasure without a new event, ice entrapment or another unnoticed slide.
