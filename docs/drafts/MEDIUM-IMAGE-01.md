## medium-image-01-01: Everyone came in early

Character: Sela Current. Subject: river. Theme: cooperation. Candidate, needs lead text review.

I tried to begin with the little stones. A light rattling, then the hollow sound under the bank, then the deep rush around the boulder. I had the whole thing arranged.

The boulder came in immediately.

He always does. I can arrive softly or throw myself against him with half a hillside in me. He produces the same enormous opinion. The little stones have to tumble over one another to be heard.

For a while I resented this. I wanted to know what I sounded like without everybody else's hard surfaces getting involved. I spread out in a deep pool and listened.

Almost nothing.

Downstream, the boulder was roaring away with a part of me I had already sent ahead. He sounded magnificent. I went back to being cross with him. It was easier than admitting how much I had missed the noise.

Visible evidence: Turquoise water moves around many pale boulders and smaller submerged stones. No people or animals are resolved. The imagined arrangement of sounds and resentment are fiction; no claim of actual musical composition or a conscious rock. The narrator remains distributed, so she does not reverse upstream.

Continuity: A new concern about how much of Sela's voice belongs to her surroundings. Do not reuse as another character needing an irritating companion to feel complete. Main weakness to judge: whether the last two sentences explain too much after the deep-pool contrast.

## medium-image-01-02: The edge I remember

Character: Sela Current. Subject: river. Theme: attachment and erosion. Candidate, needs lead text review.

There used to be a sharp place here. I knew it without looking. Every time I passed, it pressed into me.

I went around it in summer. In the wet months I covered it completely. I brought sand against it, then took the sand away. I knew every little hollow where the sand would hesitate. There are parts of another body you learn by returning, although I have always had difficulty explaining what returning means when you are a river.

The sharp place is gone now.

At first I blamed the winter. Then I noticed the smooth hollow exactly where I had been touching him. I have spent all this time worrying that he would change while I was away. I have never been away.

I would like to apologize without making the hollow any deeper. So far I have managed neither.

Visible evidence: Clear water rests against pale sculpted rock with a dark undercut and a pebble bed. The historical sharp edge, relationship, and apology are fictional. No erosion rate or individual geological origin is inferred. The current view supplies the worn-contact premise, not a claim that a before-and-after comparison was photographed.

Continuity: Extends attachment through a material consequence without a false death or keepsake. Do not turn Sela into a uniformly solemn narrator. Lead must judge whether the intimacy earns another river essay.

## medium-image-01-03: I was going to be quiet

Character: Sela Current. Subject: river. Theme: scale and divided awareness. Candidate, needs lead text review.

By the time I reached the narrow place, I had promised myself a quiet afternoon.

This was a decision made in a broad, comfortable part of me. There was room to spread out there. Light lay on the stones. The banks were being agreeable. I felt capable of almost anything, including restraint.

Then the gorge brought both walls close. Behind me, more of me kept arriving. Farther up, a stream was adding an opinion I had not finished hearing. I could not explain the situation to the incoming water by sending a polite ripple against the current.

I went through loudly.

The broad reach is still having a lovely afternoon. If you ask her, she will tell you how peaceful we are. I am the part wedged between two walls, carrying out the consequences of her excellent mood.

Visible evidence: A narrow rock corridor contains turbulent, pale blue water and foam. The peaceful upstream reach is a fictional contrast, not pictured. Fiction does not claim that water intentionally chooses flow rate or communicates upstream against flow. The narrator is the river system.

Continuity: Avoid the rejected visitor saying the noisy river is peaceful. Here the mistake is Sela's own geographically local conclusion about her distributed body. Possible duplication risk with earlier divided-flow material must be checked before admission.

## medium-image-01-04: Terms of proximity

Character: Ivo Softshield. Subject: soft shield fern. Theme: cooperation and boundaries. Hold for strict lead judgment.

The ivy asked whether I minded company. I said there was plenty of room below me.

Below was an important part of that sentence.

For some time we got along very well. I held my frond over the ground. The ivy occupied the ground. We were close in a way that allowed me to feel generous without changing any of my arrangements.

Then a new leaf appeared nearer my stalk. Another followed. The ivy seemed to think proximity was a direction rather than an agreement.

I began composing a firm objection. While I was deciding how to open it, the sun moved and my shadow crossed the little leaves. They looked rather good under there. I spent the rest of the afternoon pretending to inspect my own edges.

The objection remains unfinished. It is difficult to sound inhospitable while admiring what someone has done with your shade.

Visible evidence: One divided green frond stands above ground-level ivy leaves. Actual climbing or contact is not shown. Growth, dialogue, chronology, and the narrator's admiration are fiction. Do not claim that ivy is parasitic or that either plant consciously moves its leaves.

Continuity: An external neighbor, not another argument among Ivo's own fronds. This may still repeat the cast's frequent reluctant-companionship mechanism. Do not admit on image fit alone.

## medium-image-01-05: The light had other plans

Character: Ivo Softshield. Subject: soft shield fern. Theme: self-knowledge. Hold for strict lead judgment.

I had been admiring one particularly accomplished portion of myself. Green, firm, beautifully divided. The light reached every small edge. I thought I had finally grown into someone with a consistent character.

Then a shadow crossed the frond.

The same surface became darker, heavier, faintly severe. Nothing in me had changed enough to deserve this much difference. I waited. The bright patch moved farther along, and a part I had barely considered began looking exceptional.

I tried to remember which section had seemed so impressive first. They were all there. The little brown marks had been there too. I had been admiring the light and taking most of the credit.

I would like to say this has made me less vain. At present I am waiting for the bright patch to come back before discussing the matter further.

Visible evidence: A mature frond crosses alternating sun and shade; a few small brown marks are visible, with no diagnosed cause. Moving light and self-appraisal are fictional context. No voluntary fern movement or visual nervous system is asserted.

Continuity: This avoids counting his subdivisions and reproductive inspection. Risk: it can read like a human vanity parable in a fern costume. Keep only if the photograph and imperfect admission earn the reflection; otherwise reject.

## medium-image-01-06: Something worth chewing over

Character: Peri Bracken. Subject: peacock butterfly larva. Theme: appetite and metamorphosis. Candidate, needs lead text review.

Before I had wings, Pip asked what I thought we would become.

I was eating. This was not an unusual time to find me, but he asked with enough seriousness that I stopped. He said he had heard there would be flying. I tried to imagine leaving a leaf without first reaching the end of it. The idea was so extravagant that I took another bite to steady myself.

We discussed where we would go. Up was popular. Beyond the next nettle received considerable support. I suggested taking some food, which Pip thought missed the point.

I remember being offended. We were growing these improbable future bodies out of nettle, mouthful by mouthful, and apparently I was the one being unimaginative.

I can fly now. Pip was right about the leaving.

He would be unbearable if he knew how often I land somewhere because it has food.

Visible evidence: One black, white-spotted, spiny caterpillar curves along and gnaws a nettle leaf. Pip is explicitly a remembered companion and is not claimed visible. Adult recollection of larval conversation is fiction, not a finding about autobiographical memory across metamorphosis.

Continuity: Preserve Peri's optimistic appetite and Pip as memory. This is not the accepted crowded-nettle event or the rejected leaf-fragment-on-foot plot. Chronology must explicitly remain an adult recollection.

## medium-image-01-07: The side nobody compliments

Character: Peri Bracken. Subject: peacock butterfly. Theme: privacy and relief. Candidate, needs lead text review.

I folded my wings and became much less interesting.

There was a brown surface where all that color had been. It looked worn even where it wasn't. The bright eyes disappeared together, and I could rest on a blade of grass without presenting quite so much of an occasion.

I had expected to miss the attention. Instead I noticed the grass moving under my feet. One small bend, then another. There was enough warmth left to stay. I did not need to find the next flower immediately, or show anyone that I knew where it was.

For a while I felt wonderfully unimportant.

Then the sun came out properly. I opened my wings before I had finished being modest. All four eyes at once. I would like the record to show that the sunlight started it.

Visible evidence: A perched adult shows its dark closed-wing underside on grass. The later opening is an explicitly narrated subsequent event, not photographed. The premise is the visible contrast between the cryptic underside and the character's established bright upper wings, not a claimed predator attack.

Continuity: A respite from Peri's search for the next pleasure, ending with spontaneous pleasure rather than an inspirational lesson. Do not repeat the existing attraction scene or eyespot defense. Lead should check the ending against other blame-the-sun jokes.

## medium-image-01-08: Please leave me in here

Character: Ludo Bluecap. Subject: pixie's parasol. Theme: scale and animism. Candidate, needs lead text review.

A visitor offered to get me out of the wood.

I had brought a small blue part of myself into the air, and apparently this looked like the beginning of an escape. The visitor asked whether the rest of me was stuck.

I tried to explain that the rest of me was busy. There was a great deal happening inside the wood. I had been branching through the dark for some time, and finding it much more accommodating than it looked from outside.

The visitor said it must be lonely in there.

I had not been lonely until someone began pitying the best part of my life. Now I wanted company urgently, but company that could follow me in without first insisting I come out.

He asked what I did all day.

I told him I was eating the wood. He asked when I would be finished. I said it would take longer if he kept interrupting.

Visible evidence: A pale blue cap projects on one curved attached stem from a dark vertical wood surface, with black space below and beside it. The visitor and proposed rescue are fiction and are not pictured. The physical premise is a fruiting structure attached to wood, rather than a whole mushroom-person waiting to leave. Internal hyphae are not visible in this image. No actual fungal loneliness or speech is asserted.

Continuity: Ludo remains attached and saprotrophic. This replaces an initial draft discarded during self-review for repeating the admitted log-and-bird-scale mechanism. The turn concerns a well-intended rescue that misunderstands fungal embodiment. No tree-memory dialogue is repeated. Lead must judge whether the offscreen visitor is adequately supported by the very clear attached-body photograph.
