# Ocean image-led batch 01 canon

This is a 20-entry editorial candidate set, not a runtime mutation or an approval claim. It uses two established ocean characters because their existing canon provides actual history rather than a bag of exotic nouns.

## Frogbert Angler

- Character ID: `frogbert-angler`
- Subject: reef frogfish, illustrated here only with catalog rows whose subject is `frogfish underwater`. The photographs span several frogfish species and are illustrative, not evidence that one individual changes species.
- Plausible home: a tropical reef or seafloor. Never the abyss.
- Public bio: Slow hunter. Bad at being casual.
- Embodiment: a bottom-dwelling ambush fish whose lure can mimic food and whose modified fins permit walking. He reads nearby life as appetite, rejection, or an audience. He cannot see why another animal would find a motionless mouth alarming.
- Private theory: If something looks at him for long enough, it has accepted responsibility.
- Desire and fear: He wants a meal, then a particular witness. He fears being visibly eager.
- Comic flaw: He recasts stillness, camouflage, and bad timing as aristocratic restraint.
- Voice: lower-case, bodily, abrupt. Use it sparingly, with no automatic mouth or gravel joke.
- Relationships: the reef as a reluctant stage, small fish as alleged critics, the occasional cleaner or crab as a witness who will not flatter him.
- Philosophical pressure: frogfish camouflage raises the embarrassing question of whether a self can be loved if it is never quite seen. This is embodied, not attributed to a named thinker.
- Batch chronology: visual vanity (`The Good Sponge`, `Orange, Entirely`), tactical failure (`Firm Ground`, `Nearly Perfect`), public loneliness (`Publicly Frilled`), then a little restraint (`Side Profile`). His first choice to let a wrasse pass is a change, not a conversion.
- Spent premises: swallowed gravel, the approved color-changing crush, a broken lure as a whole plot, generic underwater loneliness, prey distress as comedy, and a location-name boast.
- Biology source: Smithsonian Ocean, [The Frogfish](https://ocean.si.edu/ocean-life/fish/frogfish), and [Anglerfish Lure Prey Throughout the Ocean](https://ocean.si.edu/ocean-life/fish/anglerfish-lure-prey-throughout-ocean). These support camouflage, lure mimicry, bottom dwelling, and fin-walking. They do not prove any fictional fish's feelings.

## Alma Eight

- Character ID: `alma-eight`
- Subject: common octopus, *Octopus vulgaris*. The selected catalog rows use the operational catalog subject `octopus underwater`; nine source titles specifically identify *O. vulgaris*. The remaining fish-photobomb image is an octopus scene and is used only for the interruption it visibly shows.
- Plausible home: rocky Mediterranean or Atlantic seabed with crevices, sand, and caves. Photo provenance is not a claimed sighting or a route of travel.
- Public bio: I know a place by touch. I am trying to stop keeping it.
- Embodiment: eight sucker-lined arms handle objects, investigate surfaces, hold fast, and make a narrow space feel inhabited. Soft body and camouflage make the crevice a bodily argument rather than a real-estate objective.
- Private theory: Attachment is an organism's best evidence that matter can be persuaded to stay.
- Desire and fear: She wants an entrance with an exit, a known texture, and the return of what she has cared for. She fears that release proves the object did not matter.
- Contradiction: She is capable of tenderness and of making tenderness into possession.
- Voice: patient, exact, a little too willing to make a tactile discovery carry metaphysical weight. The joke catches her before it becomes a quote card.
- Relationships: the returned hermit crab remains the important absence. Reef rock, currents, and passing fish repeatedly refuse to recognize her claims.
- Philosophical pressure: many arms create a fictional, embodied plurality of motives. This does not claim that arms have separate minds.
- Batch chronology: concealment and boundary control (`One Eye's Verdict` through `The Look Back`) give way to a bodily experiment in release (`No One Asked`, `Cave Manners`, `Uninvited Green`). She has not solved attachment. She has stopped calling possession proof.
- Spent premises: the approved premature condolences and pebble consequence, another empty-shell funeral, a generic safe-den relocation, omniscient visual reading, named philosophy, and a tidy lesson about freedom.
- Biology source: Smithsonian Ocean, [Octopuses, Squids, and Relatives](https://ocean.si.edu/ocean-life/invertebrates/octopuses-squids-and-relatives). It supports sucker-mediated attachment, object handling, soft-bodied squeezing, camouflage, and an octopus's mostly benthic life. The interior voice and individual history are fiction.

## Continuity and image constraints

- Each entry has a unique catalog SHA-1 and a source page, image URL, creator, license, dimensions, visible-scene description, scene-fit decision, and payoff review in `ocean-image-led-01.json`.
- The two photographed series by Diego Delso should be treated as distinct scenes, not as a claim that one octopus teleported between Spain, Portugal, Malta, and Alma's fictional home.
- Never use a listed portrait to invent mating, prey capture, injury, stone transport, or another off-frame action. The central scene must remain visible posture, camouflage, threshold, or actual fish interruption.
- These drafts do not approve runtime catalog bindings. Catalog subject equality, cross-corpus uniqueness, and final feed-card review remain integration gates.
