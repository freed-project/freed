## Vesta Prickle: an intimidating mother

Species and home remain *Extatosoma tiaratum*, woodland near Brisbane, Australia, -27.44, 152.95. She remains a herbivore. Her earlier successful bluff against beetles is remembered in the ant story and thorn incident, but neither episode simply repeats that success.

Four new events:
1. An ant carries one of her eggs away. Her threatening posture cannot control the situation. She ends up asking a stranger to convey thanks to another stranger.
2. A smaller male holds onto her while advising her to let go. Retain the approved short exactly, without explaining it.
3. A thorn ignores her declaration that they are on the same side. She recognizes something of the beetles' experience of her.
4. A mantis judges her herbivory. She asks how his own morning is going rather than accepting his definition of courage.

The first egg episode can precede the male encounter. Facultative parthenogenesis permits a daughter without a prior mate; no claim is made that all eggs in every clutch are female. The male is a fictional recurring acquaintance, privately called Ash. He is affectionate, physically clingy and more fluent in advice than self-observation. Do not turn every Ash episode into sex, grip jokes or incompetence.

The mother wants certainty that the ant cannot give her. Her fear is not a setup for harmed offspring. Future development can let the unnamed ant or her colony become a trusted neighbor. Vesta never physically handles the egg with invented hands.

[Taronga's spiny leaf insect account](https://www.taronga.org.au/about/newsroom/posts/meet-the-masters-of-disguise/) documents parthenogenesis and ant transport of eggs for the capitulum. [NSW's phasmid fact sheet](https://fieldofmar-e.schools.nsw.gov.au/fact-sheets/invertebrates/phasmids-fact-sheet) explains the egg and ant-associated nymph. Dialogue, gratitude and parental worry are fiction, not observations of this species' behavior.

Photo limits: the egg image is an arranged macro on a pale surface, not a forest floor. The ant is not photographed. The male attachment image visibly contains two insects; it does not prove every foot is in contact. All six feet is the character's fictional description of his grip, consistent with insect anatomy. The thorn photograph shows spines and thorns but not an actual snag. The hanging portrait does not contain a mantis.

## Ottilie Hook: a larger world

Species and home remain *Curculio glandium*, English oak woodland, 51.77, -1.32. Her frankness does not depend on four rostrum jokes.

Four new events:
1. As an adult standing on an acorn, she remembers her legless larval life inside one, the hole she chewed, and her initial retreat from the enormous outside.
2. She and another female try an awkward goodbye kiss. The source explicitly identifies two females. The relationship may remain affectionate or romantic; no forced label is added.
3. She spends too long planning a route around a bark crack before remembering flight.
4. She protects an afternoon of rest by declining a male's offer of company.

Private acquaintance: the other female is May, patient in most situations but liable to follow Ottilie's movement when trying to be helpful. They already like each other; do not make the next four entries about admitting it. The resting scene's male is not May and does not need a new public identity.

Her larval memories are invented autobiography. The photographed adult has legs. Do not use its portrait as evidence of a legless adult. [Wildlife Trusts](https://www.wildlifetrusts.org/wildlife-explorer/invertebrates/beetles/acorn-weevil) describes acorn-associated development. The [Darwin Tree of Life genome note](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13096787/) and [oviposition study](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-2818/14/11/922) document female acorn use. The larval exit is not mother-sized drilling: larvae chew their way out and subsequently develop in soil.

Photo limits: the goodbye image has a pale blue artificial surface, not a leaf. Its source identifies two females. Sex is not independently established for the other three photos. They are species-identified source portraits, not sexed biological records. Source locality never substitutes for the fictional home.

## Dora Dew: seasons and curiosity

Species and home remain *Drosera rotundifolia*, Rhön bog, Germany, 50.5193, 10.0625. Appetite is not the whole personality. This batch gives her fear, curiosity, indignation and a petty social argument.

Four new events:
1. She prepares for dormancy and asks the moss what happens if she does not wake. The moss offers to grow around her either way. She asks him to leave room.
2. Bea describes a lake and flying beyond it. Dora envies mobility; the traveler's pleasure in Dora's flower complicates that envy without pretending Dora is not missing anything.
3. Dora reads Darwin's experiments on her species and wants to reverse the observer/subject roles.
4. A neighboring sundew accuses her of never visiting. She asks for a demonstration.

The moss is a persistent private relationship, not a specified species. Its unsentimental response is neither a threat nor a botanical claim that moss must kill sundews. The neighbor remains a separate unnamed sundew, not Dora talking to another leaf of herself. Bea remains an independent fictional bee, not a promised winter survivor; no lifespan or exact species is assigned.

[USDA's species review](https://research.fs.usda.gov/feis/species-reviews/drorot) documents autumn hibernaculum formation. The inspected photo shows the compact bud, old leaves and green moss. This is living dormancy, not a dead specimen. The story's fear and conversations are imagined.

[Charles Darwin, Insectivorous Plants, 1875](https://darwin-online.org.uk/converted/published/1875_Insectivorous_F1217/1875_Insect_F1217.html), is the primary source for the historical reference. The Drosera chapters describe meat, glass and cheese trials. No quotation is attributed to Darwin. Dora's proposed response is fictional. This named intellectual reference stays on LinkedIn. It proposes placing things on him, not harming or consuming him.

The flower photo contains no bee, lake or flight. Its role is the specific sensory object that an imagined mobile visitor enjoys. The paired rosettes visibly support two neighboring rooted plants. The leaf macro illustrates the actual glandular structures studied by Darwin, not a historical laboratory reconstruction.
