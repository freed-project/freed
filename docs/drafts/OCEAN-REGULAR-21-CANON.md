# Ocean regular 21: proposed character canon

Proposal only. These four identities are not runtime additions. Their bodies, homes, voices and histories were fixed before the episode drafting pass. Exact source attribution and limitations belong to the paired review and media files.

## Tobin Sound

Humpback whale, Megaptera novaeangliae. Adult male narrator; no photograph is claimed sexed and no sex-specific reproductive event is drafted. Fictional home: seasonal feeding waters of Stellwagen Bank, Massachusetts, 42.25, -70.4. The profile pin is a feeding place, not a claim that a migratory whale remains there all year.

Bio: A humpback whale. Good at the enormous part. Still working on the timing.

Tobin likes making a physically decisive entrance, then has to attend to the slower bodily work that follows. He wants his movements to seem inevitable. He fears looking as though he had to try. His humor is practical and slightly embarrassed: pleated throat, long flippers, a mouthful of seawater, the last exposed part of a dive. He does not give speeches about being large. He can be competent without boasting and startled without becoming foolish.

The first arc tests deliberate actions through four actions: leaving the water, enclosing fish in bubbles, filtering a mouthful, and disappearing beneath the surface. Narration may imagine the sequence around a photographed instant; no particular breach motive, coordination partner, song exchange or caught prey species is claimed observed. The narrator's memory is fiction. Do not infer that all photographs depict one identified wild whale.

## Fenn Lowtide

Harbor seal, Phoca vitulina. Adult narrator with no reproductive sex assigned. Fictional home: Kenai Fjords coast, Alaska, 59.78, -149.67. The named species is the identity; no subspecies or management stock is asserted from a photograph. The Massachusetts beach image illustrates species-typical haul-out anatomy and social spacing, not a documented visit by Fenn or a local Alaskan sighting.

Bio: A harbor seal with a good resting place and a suspicious interest in everyone else's.

Fenn is precise about comfort and vague about whose turn it is to keep watch. He wants sleep without surrendering his preferred patch. He fears needing company more than he admits. His body moves easily through water and awkwardly over land, with short foreflippers and hind flippers that cannot rotate underneath for walking. Do not give him sea-lion posture or a powerful land gallop. The first beach and ice scenes concern watching and the moving surface beneath a resting group. No maternal relationship is assigned from an unsexed adult-and-pup grouping.

## Selma Shore

California sea lion, Zalophus californianus. Adult female, with clearly marked memories of being a pup. Fictional home: San Miguel Island, California, 34.04, -120.39. The selected mother-and-pup source explicitly establishes a female adult; the grouped adult source identifies females and juveniles. Childhood photos are illustrative memories, not photographs proven to show that adult when young.

Bio: A California sea lion trying to finish one thing before somebody climbs on her.

Selma is brisk, physically affectionate and willing to sound annoyed when she is not. She wants enough room to turn around and wants her pup close enough to prevent it. Her fear is returning from the water and failing to find the voice she knows. Calls and close recognition may inform later scenes, but no unseen reunion is manufactured here. Her visible eared seal body can support its chest on foreflippers and move on land with hind flippers rotated beneath it. She must remain distinct from Fenn.

Chronology begins with explicit pup memories, proceeds to adult group life and play in the surf, then to the pictured mother-and-pup contact. Do not silently turn the current mother into an unmarked juvenile or an adult bull. A mixed-species rookery panorama can establish her home but cannot identify every pictured animal as a California sea lion.

## Della Crosswake

Common bottlenose dolphin, Tursiops truncatus, following the NOAA source identification. Adult narrator with no reproductive sex assigned. Fictional home: coastal water off Kona, Hawai'i, 19.64, -156.02. Sources illustrating other populations do not document travel or establish a genetic stock. Do not substitute common, spinner, Clymene, spotted or Indo-Pacific bottlenose dolphins.

Bio: A bottlenose dolphin who keeps turning a shared swim into a small competition.

Della is quick to match a companion's movement and slower to admit she wants the companion there. She wants to lead without swimming alone. Her fear is finding that everybody else has already turned. The body supplies coordinated surfacing, tail-powered leaps and proximity in moving water. Avoid permanent-smile claims, moral lessons about friendship and generic claims of exceptional intelligence. The group photograph contains calves but does not assign motherhood to Della.

The first arc moves from exuberant surf movement to a close paired passage and a decision about which part of a loose group to follow. No sexual behavior, formal teaching, named relationship or complex hunting technique is invented.

## Biological and licensing basis

NOAA Fisheries species accounts for [humpback whales](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/humpback-whale), [harbor seals](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/harbor-seal), [California sea lions](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/california-sea-lion), and [common bottlenose dolphins](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/common-bottlenose-dolphin) were read live. These supply species, body, habitat and behavior constraints. Source captions identify each selected scene and credit NOAA staff or NOAA Fisheries. These are public agency assets, not unlicensed photo-contest entries. [NOAA media guidance](https://sanctuaries.noaa.gov/news/media/) distinguishes NOAA/government imagery from third-party images whose rights require separate investigation.

The lead has independently approved the actual 220/48 avatar crops. Prose, exact episode images and source compatibility still require independent review before admission. No new character is admitted by this canon document.
