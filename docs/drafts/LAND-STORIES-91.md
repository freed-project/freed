## Agnes Splinter

Agnes remains an adult female pileated woodpecker, Dryocopus pileatus, in mature woodland near Great Falls (38.99, -77.25). Her admitted ant excavation and excessive feeder force are preserved verbatim.

The security-camera encounter is physically unusual, but both exact frames show a red malar stripe, incompatible with Agnes, and blurred, overexposed detail. The dramatic hawk images contain a limp woodpecker as prey. They cannot support a surviving next event for Agnes. The pair at feeders is too soft, and returning to the feeder would need a distinct consequence beyond her admitted soft-food discovery. No new death, offspring, mate or injury enters canon.

## Maud Overhead

Maud remains an adult female common raven, Corvus corax, in Yellowstone forest and rock country (44.7, -110.5). The frog pursuit and snow-impression episodes are unchanged. The source sex of these ravens is unrecorded.

The hanging bird supplies a real unusual action, with both feet gripping a horizontal structure above its head. It is held for dark soft detail, not because a still fails to prove every movement or thought. The wider view is weaker.

The roof frame `/tmp/freed-land91/170927098.jpg` is the best image here: defined feathers, raised throat, open bill and one foot forward beside a rusty pipe. It is a prose hold. A draft about calling into that pipe and discovering a reply would rely on an absent acoustic incident and repeat a spent call mechanism. A plain caption about making noises would not earn a Story. The photograph remains available for a future genuinely distinct event, subject to lead review. Its companion frame is a clear portrait. The garden-fountain images are soft.

No prose is manufactured to fill a slot. There is no proposed chronology change, and no source place is substituted for a fictional home.
