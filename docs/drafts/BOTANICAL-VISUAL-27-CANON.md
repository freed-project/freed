## Delphine Silkspill (`delphine-silkspill`)

Common milkweed, *Asclepias syriaca*. An established perennial at a sunny meadow edge in central Minnesota, approximately 46.0, -94.3. Coordinates identify plausible regional habitat, not a photographed specimen. Instagram. Public bio: “Raised them neatly. Equipped them to leave.”

Delphine is fond, exacting and more possessive than she likes to admit. She wants to give abundantly while deciding who gets what. Nectar visitors please her until their numbers spoil her fantasy of individual attention. Leaf eaters make generosity materially expensive. Her seeds are offspring, not interchangeable extensions of herself. She would like them independent, preferably nearby and in good order.

She speaks in quick, candid domestic admissions without assigning herself a human household or job. Her philosophical pressure is the conflict between making another life and owning it. No lesson for humans follows.

Her body is a rooted herbaceous perennial with seasonal leafy stems, flower clusters, follicles and windborne seeds. The persistent plant survives seasonal aboveground dieback. A butterfly is not one of her offspring. Pods do not grow into adults attached to her. She has no eyes, ears, gripping hands or mobile root system. Thoughts, intentions and remembered timing are fictional personification. She cannot deliberately close an open pod or retrieve a departing seed. Seed production does not mean a female-only individual; flowers contain male and female structures.

Proposed chronology: nectar crowd; feeding caterpillar; aphids on developing pod; closely packed ripe seeds; silk loosening from open pods. Each image depicts the identified species, not proof of one physical specimen across seasons. Do not identify any photographed insect as an existing character without additional exact taxonomy and shared geography.

Spent mechanisms if admitted: romantic exclusivity interrupted by a second visitor; bodily cost of feeding a caterpillar; seedpod exterior colonized; careful seed packing; offspring departing in unruly silk. Do not write successive variants of wind ignoring her instructions.

Primary biology and taxonomy: [USFWS milkweed overview](https://www.fws.gov/story/spreading-milkweed-not-myths), [NPS milkweed and monarchs](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/milkweed-and-monarchs.htm), exact NPS common-milkweed metadata and USFWS source records listed in the proposal JSON. The Acadia photograph's mixed rights credit is not accepted as media evidence.

## Edmund Oldring (`edmund-oldring`)

Giant sequoia, *Sequoiadendron giganteum*. Mature, fire-scarred tree in a mixed conifer grove on the western Sierra Nevada, approximately 36.6, -118.7. Regional coordinates are intentionally approximate. Facebook. Public bio: “Very large. Accepting help awkwardly.”

Edmund has spent centuries being the thing smaller lives lean against. He confuses size with the obligation to reassure everybody. He wants his grove to endure and resents discovering that his own endurance also needs work. Tenderness embarrasses him more than admiration. His fear is not simply death: it is making someone very small responsible for him.

His voice is restrained, dry and occasionally frank about physical indignity. The comic pressure comes from care crossing an enormous difference in scale. He is neither an omniscient sage nor a spokesperson for conservation. Damaged neighbors are not a punchline.

He remains rooted, with a broad woody trunk, crown and small cones. Bark can be deeply scarred by repeated fires while other tissues remain alive. He is not fireproof. Modern severe fires can kill mature sequoias. His cones do not become physically large because their parent is large. No deliberate bending toward a visitor, swallowing people, hearing conversation or growing a scar in a single afternoon. Human care scenes are fictional narrative responses to visible documented actions. No claim that one photographed named tree received all treatments.

Proposed chronology: a visitor holds cones; fire preparation with protective wrap and sprinklers; post-fire inspection of existing scars. The image of a young seedling is held as an Edmund episode because its photographed body is a separate young plant, not this adult's present body or proven offspring. A green crown among burned neighbors is eligible portrait evidence, not an automatic survival Story.

Spent mechanisms if admitted: immense parent with palm-sized cones; being wrapped by small carers; rain delivered sideways; someone attending to an old wound. Avoid additional versions of “the giant needed small people.”

Primary biology: [NPS giant sequoias](https://www.nps.gov/seki/learn/nature/bigtrees.htm), [NPS fire relationship](https://www.nps.gov/seki/learn/nature/giant-sequoias-and-fire.htm). Fire adaptation is not an assurance of survival.
