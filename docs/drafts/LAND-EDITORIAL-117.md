It held.

I pulled myself toward the wood, then stopped. My feet were pulling at grass. The grass was pulling at more grass. Somewhere above me was the little join I'd nearly thrown away.

Let go with one foot. Put it back. Tried the other.

A loose end brushed my face. I reached to tear it out, remembered what I was hanging from, and tucked my bill shut.

### The loop that stayed

I carried a green strip to the branch and bent it around the fork. Held one end with my foot. Pulled the other with my bill. The whole thing slid free and came with me when I leaned back.

The next strip did the same. The third caught on a rough place in the bark. I tugged it, harder this time, and it held.

I hung beneath it to test the join. My weight drew it tight. The loose end tickled my face, but I left it alone while I dangled there. For the first time that morning, something I had made was holding something I was. Then I sneezed and nearly let go.

### The downward beginning

The tadpoles had become dark, wriggling shapes inside the eggs. I sat nearby while rain ticked against the leaves. One moved, then another. The clear mound began to open.

They slipped free and fell toward the stream. I followed each drop with my eyes until there were too many small movements to follow. The water received them without changing its sound.

Afterward I went across the leaf to the place where the clutch had been. It was suddenly easy to move there. I sat down, then stood, then went to the edge. I had spent so long keeping small bodies on that leaf that watching them leave it felt like failing at the thing I had been doing correctly.
