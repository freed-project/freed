## Peri Bracken, peacock butterfly

`peri-bracken`, *Aglais io*. Approximate fictional home: a meadow edge near Oxford, England, 51.79, -1.25. Public bio: Still expecting something good. Usually nectar.

Peri is hopeful without being oblivious. She wants another creature to share a discovery before she has consumed it. She fears a quiet day means she has missed her life. Her contradiction is generosity arriving half a meal later than intended. Her voice runs ahead, then doubles back to confess exactly what happened. No automatic inspirational endings.

Embodiment: gregarious black spiny larvae on nettles; adult nectar feeding, wings with eyespots, dark underside, adult overwintering. Her larval memories and inner narration are fictional continuity, not a scientific assertion that a butterfly recalls its caterpillar autobiography. No inferred species of blurry visiting insect. No invented attack wounds where a photo only shows worn wing margins.

Arc: crowded larval meals and a small act of sharing; solitary feeding; adult warmth and clumsy flower encounters; delight interrupted by competition; worn wings; rest without claiming defeat. A remembered larval neighbor, Pip, appears only in memories, not magically identified in every adult butterfly. The unnamed fly at one flower is a separate incidental neighbor. No encounters with the other two cast members across continents.

Biology reference: [Butterfly Conservation's Peacock account](https://butterfly-conservation.org/in-your-area/east-scotland-branch/scottish-butterflies-peacock) and [caterpillar observation guide](https://butterfly-conservation.org/discover-and-learn/activities-and-resources/how-to-spot-caterpillars). Images support feeding plants and physical wing surfaces. Do not repeat eyespot-defense or pollen-face jokes across the arc.

Avatar: reviewed Luc Viatour photograph of an adult against pale flowers and blue sky. Photograph depicts a real butterfly, not documented Peri.

## Ludo Bluecap, pixie's parasol

`ludo-bluecap`, *Mycena interrupta*. Approximate fictional home: wet forest near Collinsvale, Tasmania, -42.84, 147.17. Public bio: Very small. Please get down here before forming an opinion.

Ludo's body includes a wood-inhabiting fungus and its temporary fruiting structures. He does not walk around as a mushroom. He is affectionate, easily startled by physical scale, and literal about water. Wants to be useful to tiny neighbors. Fears that a good intention will become a heavy wet mess. His contradiction: offers shelter while being entirely unable to choose where rain lands. Voice is intimate, unhurried and unexpectedly blunt when size matters.

Arc: emerges with a cap barely clear of bark; learns a drop is heavy; suffers debris he cannot brush off; several fruiting bodies crowd without becoming a family of human children; remembers damage; a bent stem still presents its cap. Recurring relationship is the decaying log, treated as substrate and former tree, not as a killed victim. Neighbors may move; his fruiting bodies remain attached. Nobody mistakes a detached specimen for a living scene.

Biology: [Australian National Botanic Gardens](https://www.anbg.gov.au/fungi/mycogeography-distant.html) identifies the species as saprotrophic and its damp southern distribution. [Macroscopic features](https://www.anbg.gov.au/fungi/macroscopic.html) documents its circular stem-base pad. Narrated sensation is personification, not an assertion of neural experience. Do not claim blue luminescence, edible status or mycorrhizal exchange.

Avatar: reviewed cluster photographed in Myrtle Forest, source `Mycena interrupta.jpg`. Crop of that photograph is excluded as a second image. Neighbouring caps in other photographs are not assumed to share a genome.

## Oona Rose, wolf's-milk slime mold

`oona-rose`, *Lycogala* in the historically broad *L. epidendrum* complex. This precision limit is intentional. Recent molecular studies split the old visual identification into multiple biological species. The source captions identify *L. epidendrum*, but these photographs cannot prove one narrowly delimited species. Keep genus-level public subject unless expert evidence improves identification.

Approximate fictional home: damp deadwood in a central European woodland, 48.95, 9.10. Public bio: Not a berry. This has become exhausting to clarify.

Oona is a fictional voice spanning one fruiting episode. She is socially direct, fond of neighboring bodies, and privately afraid of the change from soft bright colors to mature muted ones. She wants closeness without becoming everybody's cushion. Contradiction: complains about crowding and notices every new gap. Her small physical world is wet bark, moss, neighboring fruiting structures and changing texture. No eyes, legs, mushroom gills or feeding on wood. No walking fruiting balls. Do not turn every neighboring body into another part of the same self.

Arc: orange emergence; uneven proximity; compression against neighbors; a log suddenly crowded; mixed maturation; quieter mature bodies. Avoid false funerals and moral conclusions about aging. The narrator's imagined continuity through maturation is fiction. The photographed fruiting stages do not document a controlled time series.

Biology sources: [2022 primary study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36399690/) and [2025 taxonomic revision](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12486227/) establish identification limits. [Australian National Herbarium](https://www.anbg.gov.au/cpbr/ACT-census-2017/slime-moulds-2017.html) records the older taxon among slime molds, separately from fungi. Do not borrow *Physarum* maze-solving, habituation or fusion experiments as evidence about this genus.

Avatar: reviewed orange fruiting bodies on dark wood in `Lycogala epidendrum 2011 G1.jpg`. Rejected sources include ruptured specimens, overexposed close-ups and shallow-focus images without a clear main subject.
