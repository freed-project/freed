## 01. Mira Mask: What my paws were doing

candidate. facebook post.

Woke with claws pressing into my palm. Pulled away and the pressure followed. My other paw had hold of this one.

I opened the first paw. The second tightened. Opened that one and nearly rolled off the branch trying to see what the first was doing.

Put my cheek back on the bark. Set one paw down, then the other. Waited until both had stopped gripping.

They crept together again as I got sleepy. I let them, with the claws out of the way.

Visible image: A raccoon lies sideways in a tree fork, cheek against bark and its forepaws gathered together in front of its face.

Fiction boundary: Self-contact and branch support are visible. Sleep, pressure, sequential unclenching, a slight roll and gentler contact are invented. The paws are not identified as a caught hind foot. Source sex and precise age are unrecorded; female narrator is established fiction. No diagnostic confusion, injury or universal raccoon behavior claimed.

## 02. Oswin Broadback: Where I put my weight

candidate. facebook post.

He pushed my head sideways until I could see grass with one eye and nothing but him with the other.

I drove harder. My back feet dug in. My front feet slid.

Had to stop trying to move him long enough to move one foot of my own. Set it farther out. Felt the ground hold. Brought my head back against his and pushed from there.

We turned a little. Neither of us went backward. I could feel him breathing through the hair against my forehead.

When we parted, I stood with my feet still spread. He stood that way too.

Visible image: Two adult bison bulls press lowered heads together in grass; the left bull has one eye visible, with other bison behind.

Fiction boundary: Adult bulls and head contact are source-supported. Foot slipping, adjustment, mutual turning, breathing sensations and aftermath are invented. No winner, mating outcome, injury, trapped horns or audience reaction is asserted. Source location Wind Cave illustrates compatible grassland without relocating the fictional Yellowstone home.

## 03. Otis Gulp: All the way open

candidate. facebook post.

Spread one wing over the water and began to open the other. A bill was in the way. Folded it again. Turned. Now the first wing brushed somebody else.

I paddled out until I could open both all the way. The wet feathers pulled apart, and a gust caught underneath them. Had to paddle harder to keep from turning.

Stayed wide until I was ready to fold them. Then went back to the others and settled close enough to touch.

Visible image: A white pelican stands or floats upright in shallow water with both wings extended, black flight feathers visible and several other white pelicans nearby.

Fiction boundary: Wing spread, water and nearby conspecifics anchor the scene. Partial opening, brushing, displacement, gust, paddling and return are invented. No takeoff, plunge diving, invisible fish, mate, offspring or documented feather-drying purpose claimed. Source identifies Pelecanus erythrorhynchos; adult plumage is visible, sex unrecorded. Male narrator remains fiction.

## 04. Maud Overhead: The second set

candidate. facebook post.

The snow gave way under my feet. I spread my wings, struck down and got back into the air with snow falling off me.

From the branch I could see where I had been. A hollow for my body. Long separate grooves on either side.

Went back and stood beside them. Lowered one wing into the untouched snow, lifted it, then lowered the other. Looked underneath. Every feather had made its own thin place.

This time I jumped toward the branch before opening my wings. Wanted to leave the second set sharp.

Visible image: A deep bird-shaped depression and clearly separated wing-feather impressions mark snow, with other tracks along the right edge; no bird is present.

Fiction boundary: The NPS record explicitly identifies common raven wing prints. The narrator making the print, failed footing, return and deliberate second impression are fiction. Only one large impression is photographed. This is an aftermath image of the central physical action, not a portrait or proof of an experiment. No universal self-recognition or cognition claim. Source sex and age are unknown; established adult female narrator remains fiction.
