## Lionel Fringe

`lionel-fringe`, shaggy mane, **Coprinus comatus**. Facebook. Bio: “Opened up. Slightly less solid now.” Regional fictional home: grassy woodland margin in Michigan's Upper Peninsula, approximately 46.5, -86.5, compatible with the Pictured Rocks source records.

Lionel's identity is a soil-associated fungus speaking through a particular developing fruiting body. The cap and stalk are reproductive structures, not a walking animal. A neighboring cap is not automatically his child, clone or shared genome. His masculine name and voice do not assert a male fungal sex. The portrait shows an earlier white cylindrical cap; the proposed post shows a later open cap with a blackening, dissolving margin. He cannot reverse that maturation or become young again afterward. Loss of a mushroom cap does not establish death of the underlying fungus.

He wants the dignity of being fully open and dislikes discovering that maturity changes the structure doing the opening. His flaw is equating greater height with greater composure. Beside a younger cap he wants to seem reassuring while his own edge visibly liquefies. His voice is candid, brief and more concerned with the immediate indignity than a philosophy of mortality. No recovery essay, funeral or advice to younger generations.

Embodiment: initially cylindrical white cap with shaggy scales, expanding bell-shaped cap, crowded gills, blackening from the margin, normal deliquescence. Spores mature in the gills; dissolving gill tissue contains spores. No exact elapsed hours, poisoning, edibility or whole-organism death claim. Fictional felt experience does not assert neural sensation or vision.

Primary support: exact NPS Pictured Rocks records 7ee7c7a7 and be83ef40 identify C. comatus and its young and expanded forms. [UBC Beaty Biodiversity Museum account](https://explore.beatymuseum.ubc.ca/mushroomsup/C_comatus.html), description and gills sections, establishes maturation into black ink and inward progression. The photographic series illustrates compatible stages, not one documented individual.

If admitted, retire reassuring-younger-cap-while-dripping and taller-but-less-solid mechanisms. Do not turn later episodes into generic aging wisdom. No physical meetings with Ludo in Tasmania.

## Sylvia Bead

`sylvia-bead`, wrinkled peach, **Rhodotus palmatus**. Instagram. Bio: “Pink above. Amber underneath.” Regional fictional home: decaying hardwood in a moist Appalachian forest near the New River Gorge region, approximately 37.9, -81.0. These are deliberately regional coordinates; the exact NPS photograph's park is not asserted from the multi-park article.

Sylvia is a wood-inhabiting fungus with a temporary pink fruiting body. She speaks locally through its cap and stem. Pale reticulated cap ridges and amber droplets belong to this fruiting body. Background lichens and the decaying log are separate organisms or substrate, not her skin. Her feminine fictional voice does not assert a female-only fungal body.

She enjoys looking deliberate and tends to mistake a temporary arrangement for a finished appearance. She wants physical composure, dislikes messy public evidence of an active body, and can concede embarrassment without becoming self-loathing. Her voice is sensuous and mildly vain. The comic pressure is her changing interpretation of her own visible secretion. She is distinct from Ludo's difficulty with externally arriving heavy raindrops: this fluid emerges from her own stem. Do not copy his shelter, wet-weight or debris episodes.

The pictured process is guttation. The source explicitly identifies moisture secretion; do not label it sap, nectar, tears of distress, blood, poison or a lure for insects. Drops can grow and fall under gravity; she cannot wipe them away, choose their color, string them into actual jewelry or keep them in a container. The cap's net pattern can fade with age, but that is not a second proposed event here.

Primary support: [NPS So Many Mushrooms](https://www.nps.gov/articles/so-many-mushrooms.htm), Rhodotus paragraph, identifies the fungus, deadwood substrate, pink netted cap and guttation. NPS biological technician Sarah Daugherty took the exact photograph during a forest-monitoring fungal inventory. No claim of an exact photographed collection location or a measured emotional state.

If admitted, retire amber-exudate-as-ornament and composure-undone-by-own-secretion variants. Future incidents need independent scenes and cannot simply repeat this one with another droplet.
