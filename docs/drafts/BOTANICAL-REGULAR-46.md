## 46-01 A different touch

Sabine Warmfold, eastern skunk cabbage, Symplocarpus foetidus. Theme `courtship`. Regular `post`.

I had been waiting for pollen. Warm inside my folded hood, I imagined it arriving on a visitor's legs. I knew what I wanted from a touch.

Then my anthers began to open.

Yellow dust gathered between my little flowers. More anthers unfolded, and the creases filled with my own pollen.

I was still in the same patch of cold ground, holding the same opening. But I had begun to want someone to leave it carrying a little of me.

I had prepared so carefully to receive. I had not rehearsed this part.

## Scene and biological boundary

The image shows small flowers on a skunk-cabbage spadix at extreme magnification. Open anthers and loose yellow pollen are visible across maroon floral parts. These are not rocks, a field of separate plants or the whole plant. The photograph is a documented laboratory macro with focus stacking; no field encounter is implied by its presentation.

The fictional episode reconstructs the transition from receptive female phase to pollen-shedding male phase. It changes what Sabine wants from a future visit. No visitor actually arrives, no pollination is proved, and pollen on her own surfaces is not treated as self-fertilization. Warmth, cold ground, remembered anticipation and desire belong to compatible fictional context. The same flower's receptive stage cannot recur after this episode.

Goodrich and Gibernau's [2024 primary study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0031942224001481?dgcid=rss_sd_all), introduction, explicitly identifies pistil-bearing female phase followed by pollen-shedding stamens. The [NPS species account](https://www.nps.gov/miss/learn/nature/skunkcabbage.htm) establishes spathe/spadix anatomy, heat production and insect transfer. No timings or thermal measurements are borrowed from Asian S. renifolius.

The intended turn is a changed sexual intention in a rooted body. The full canon was written before the literal episode and is in the companion file.
