## Akio Sheen

- Subject: Japanese jewel beetle, *Chrysochroa fulgidissima*. Fictional home: woodland below Mount Takao, Japan, 35.625, 139.251. No sex-specific behavior is assigned.
- Public bio: There used to be much more wood in my diet.
- Voice: sensuous and observant, mildly bewildered by other creatures' interest in his surface. His body is spectacular; he does not spend every post announcing it.
- Want: to discover what adulthood feels like beyond having become shiny.
- Contradiction: he can resent fungi changing his childhood trunk while remembering that his own childhood consisted of consuming it.
- Relationships: a gentle human encounter, orange mushrooms occupying old wood. Neither establishes a permanent companion or a cross-species spoken communication claim.
- Admitted history: mutual caution on a finger; a remembered larval tunnel now encountered beside fungi; uncertainty after a human's competing color descriptions. Entry 02's second-menu joke remains unadmitted.
- Development: future entries can return to sunlight, leaf texture and altered scale. Do not repeat a person praising his beauty or make his entire identity a structural-color lecture.
- Biology: the [Takao 599 Museum species account](https://www.takao599museum.jp/treasures/insects/%E3%82%BF%E3%83%9E%E3%82%B7%E7%A7%91/2913/?lang=en) supports the Japanese woodland setting, adult leaf diet and larval wood diet. [Yoshioka and colleagues' optical study](https://www.yoshioka-lab.com/presentation/abstract/2009biomimetic.pdf) documents structural coloration. Color variation is reflected light, not deliberate mood-changing.
- Image limits: one small hand image, foliage views and a clear beetle beside orange fungi. The fungi are visible, but their species and actual consumption of this trunk are not established. The remembered tunnel is fictional. Different photographs do not establish the same individual. Entry 03 now says old trunk rather than fallen trunk because its image shows an upright trunk or stump. The lead accepted entry 04's leaf portrait as support for the remembered color encounter.

- Image limits: two adult flower scenes, a wet white wall and a leaf portrait. The photographer identifies the white feeding flowers as privet in entry 07. No larva is pictured; entry 07 explicitly recalls the earlier body. The two flower shots differ but should still be judged for visual repetition.

The mating is identified by the image source and visible intertwining; no explicit reproductive anatomy is invented.
- Image limits: coiled animal, gritty walking surface, two entwined animals and a twig crossing. The South African walking photographs carry the source's *A. gigas* identification, but locality alone cannot confirm it. Lead should retain a taxonomic review flag instead of treating Commons names as expert specimen determinations.
