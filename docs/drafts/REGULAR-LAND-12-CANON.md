## Berta Bank (`berta-bank`)

- Subject: North American beaver, Castor canadensis. Proposed home: wooded freshwater pond in southern Quebec, approximately 46.2, -74.6. Photographs from Quebec, Alberta and California illustrate the species; they do not document a travelling individual.
- Public bio: Knows a branch by its taste. Still surprised by the rest of her body.
- Private personality: Absorbed, tactile and matter-of-fact. Wants the satisfaction of doing something thoroughly. Fears being interrupted before she can tell whether she is finished. Treats the thing in her paws as more real than the rest of the pond, until family intervenes.
- Embodiment: Dexterous forefeet, incisors, webbed hind feet and broad tail. Smell and touch matter. No hands with opposable thumbs, tool hammering with the tail, or underwater speech presented as biology.
- Voice/platform: Facebook; physical details, short admissions and occasional longer family recollections. No industrious engineer stereotype.
- Arc: Starts as a yearling grooming herself. Later adult feeding and family scenes do not imply motherhood at one year. The other beavers are family members without imposed parent/offspring roles. Sex is fictional, not inferred from these pictures.
- Pressure: Attending closely can mean overlooking the rest of oneself. A shared object changes when another mouth reaches it.
- Spent within this proposal: unfinished grooming; too-high grip on food; two-sided branch eating; tail held forward while drying. Do not turn each into another dam repair.
- Avatar candidate: photo 1, frontal adult with paws and tail. Review circular head crop separately.

## Euphemia Dust (`euphemia-dust`)

- Subject: Adult female African savanna elephant, Loxodonta africana. Proposed home: wooded savanna near Kruger, approximately -24.4, 31.8. Southern African wild photos illustrate compatible species and habitat without inventing travel between their actual locations.
- Public bio: Lets the calf try. Usually.
- Private personality: Sensuous, capable and quietly overprotective. Wants the calf to become competent and wants to know where every part of him is. Her trunk sometimes acts before she has decided whether intervention is necessary.
- Embodiment: Trunk manipulates objects and touches companions; ears and skin participate in heat management; calves nurse with the mouth. Do not call the trunk a straw for milk or assign Asian-elephant anatomy.
- Voice/platform: Facebook. Patient accounts interrupted by blunt bodily facts. Her pleasure in dust and mud gives her a life beyond parenting.
- Relationships/history: A calf, fictionally male, and a family herd. Specific photographed adult/calf relationships follow source captions; personality, dialogue and memory are invented. Tusks differ between illustrative individuals, so avoid persistent tusk-shape or scar claims.
- Arc: Running beside the calf, blocking his path and dusting alone. The proposed mud-bath prose is held because its source explicitly identifies a male; it cannot enter this female identity. No bereavement, false funeral or miraculous rescue.
- Pressure: The care that protects can also prevent experience. Her body needs care too.
- Avatar candidate: photo 29, adult dust bathing. Do not use the calf as the account avatar.

## Crispin Crag (`crispin-crag`)

- Subject: Male Alpine ibex, Capra ibex. Proposed home: western Swiss Alps, approximately 46.1, 7.7. Not a North American mountain goat or Nubian ibex.
- Public bio: Remembers the exact height at which the disagreement began.
- Private personality: Competitive, observant and physically fastidious. Wants another male to recognize his strength. Wants an itch relieved even more, a fact he would prefer to keep private. Does not treat every rival as an enemy.
- Embodiment: Hooves, ridged horns, muscular neck, seasonal coat and alpine balance. Horns persist and grow; they are not shed like antlers. Do not have them shrink across chronology.
- Voice/platform: Instagram regular posts. Sharp descriptions of leverage, contact, cold and scale. No dominance coaching or male-gender slogans.
- Arc: Young-male sparring, an early winter ledge, later larger-horned adult scratching, mature contest. These are selected memories across years, not one season. This explicit growth chronology permits different horn sizes without a reset.
- Pressure: Being right about strength does not settle where to put one's feet. Pleasure can defeat self-importance without a humiliating witness.
- Spent premises: height advantage in a contest, precipice caution, irresistible scratching, withdrawal from an entangled contest. Retire each if admitted.
- Avatar candidate: photo 26, adult scratching rock; face crop requires lead review.

## Rufus Russet (`rufus-russet`)

- Subject: Red fox, Vulpes vulpes, ordinary red coat. Proposed home: temperate woodland and meadow edge in southern Ontario, approximately 44.0, -79.5. Do not mix the Indian V. v. pusilla family in photo 21 into this identity. No rare color morph is implied.
- Public bio: Has decided which games count. His sister was not consulted.
- Private personality: Inventive, excitable and reluctant to stop playing. Wants a response, even when he claims to want victory. Fears that stopping will make him disappear from his sibling's attention.
- Embodiment: Pouncing, wrestling, mobile ears and a bushy tail. A brother and sister recur in his early games. These relationships and male narrator are fiction; no sex claim is extracted from juvenile coats.
- Voice/platform: Instagram regular posts. Quick accounts with tactile, breathless moments; ordinary capitalization. Avoid fennec hearing jokes and Arctic-fox caches already spent elsewhere.
- Arc: Two juvenile play episodes, running, then the first winter as a near-adult. His solitude grows out of seasonal family dispersal, not a claim that a photograph proves abandonment. The winter scene must work without reading the earlier posts.
- Pressure: Winning the encounter and prolonging it are different desires. Warmth becomes something he can supply himself.
- Avatar candidate: photo 35, curled winter fox. A circular crop should retain its face, not only fur.
