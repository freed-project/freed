## Rules learned from these drafts

1. A review paragraph calling something funny is not evidence of a joke. Read the body without that paragraph.
2. Specific physical action can still be mundane. Length and ecological accuracy cannot rescue a scene with no consequence.
3. Personification permits fictional interior life, not arbitrary anatomy. An octopus cannot lift a rock ceiling to admit a visitor merely because the plot needs it.
4. Do not turn an editor's example into multiple near-identical entries. It illustrates a mechanism, not reusable copy.
5. Image-first writing must notice the image's unusual objects and relationships. Calling a discarded tire a cave throws away both accuracy and a potentially distinctive premise.
6. Different species cannot silently portray one recurring individual. Match morphology and species, or establish a different character.
