## 01. Before either of us

Mira Mask. Facebook visual Story. Theme: social. Candidate, independent review pending. An explicitly remembered juvenile event, not a present adult suddenly becoming smaller.

When I was small, I offered to help another youngster out of the water. He climbed onto my back. I was still working on the part where I got out.

Visible: two wet young raccoons at the water's edge; the rear animal presses over the lower animal's shoulders, while the lower animal braces against rock with a visible forepaw. The source explicitly describes two young raccoons. Their proximity, wet coats and rock supply the predicament. Fiction: Mira's identity, the offer, the climb and her unfinished attempt to leave the water. Neither sex nor kinship is photographically established. The other youngster is not automatically her recurring adult crossing companion.

Image judgment: strong. Warm fur, clear eyes, green water and the stacked bodies make the situation legible. Retain both animals and the lower forepaw in any Story crop. Prose judgment: promising; the offered competence meets an immediate, bodily cost. This is a childhood overpromise, not another adult reciprocal-crossing episode.

Credit correction: the Commons API chooses a public-domain template, but the page also supplies a Flickr-reviewed CC BY 2.0 license and explicitly credits Gary Miller. The manifest uses that attribution license and preserves Gary Miller / USFWS Mountain-Prairie. Do not reduce the credit to the account name or infer that Miller was a federal employee.

## 02. All mine already

Bramble Shortlegs. Instagram visual Story. Theme: feeding. Candidate, independent review pending. Proposed replacement for provisional episode 2, “Very particular grass”; no other entry depends on its dusty-grass excursion.

The other wombat kept edging toward my patch. I ate faster to keep it from him. Now he's on the good grass and I'm full.

Visible: two common wombats grazing beside each other on short grass; one is somewhat smaller. Fiction: the encroachment, Bramble's acceleration, fullness, the other animal's gender and the relative quality of the remaining grass. The source does not identify a mother or offspring, so this does not extend the newly admitted Mother memory or identify a new child. If a gendered incidental visitor adds needless canon weight, the lead may use “them” and “they're” consistently before admission.

Image judgment: clear and warm, though less visually unusual than 01 or 03. The two lowered heads must remain visible together. Prose judgment: the defensive act consumes the very appetite needed to enjoy winning. The territorial flaw moves outdoors without another burrow repair or sentimental reversal. Hold if the lead finds the photograph too ordinary despite the paired grazing scene.

## 03. The remaining leg

Calder Mugger. Facebook visual Story. Theme: wonder. Candidate, independent review pending. A new scene after the accepted foundations episodes.

I was trying to sleep when the birds started folding themselves. One leg gone, then a whole head. I have been lying here waiting to see what happens to the other leg.

Visible: a mugger crocodile with its head on a rock and most of its body at the waterline; several large birds stand on the rock, including birds with heads tucked and birds resting on one visible leg. Others remain upright. Fiction: the sequence, Calder's curiosity and his expectations. “Gone” describes his comic reading of folding, not vanished anatomy. The photograph does not document the individual birds changing pose. The source caption names painted storks; the public prose deliberately says birds because not every bird in the frame needs a taxonomic assignment.

Image judgment: strong scale and relationship, with pale stone, dark water and a complete shared scene. A tight crocodile crop would destroy it. Prose judgment: curiosity interrupts his habitual rest. The birds are neither prey suffering for a joke nor an excuse to repeat his frightening teeth or denied wish for company. His attention to an unfamiliar body carries the turn.

## 04. My own visitor

Dora Dew. Instagram visual Story. Theme: wonder. Hold for prose.

I opened a flower above the moss. He asked what it looked like. Five white petals, I told him, and then wished somebody would describe them to me.

Visible: a sharp, five-petaled white Drosera rotundifolia flower with unopened buds on its green stalk. The moss and basal leaves are outside this crop. Fiction: conversation and Dora's wish. No eyes or scientific claim of plant sight are introduced; her imagined knowledge and curiosity are personification.

Image judgment: beautiful, with the whole flower and neighboring bud legible. Prose hold: the ending almost becomes a prepared thought about self-knowledge. The central moss exchange is absent, and the plant does not do enough in the text. Do not admit this merely because the flower is lovely. It must also avoid repeating Bea's already admitted admiration of Dora's flower.

## 05. Below the outside

Ludo Bluecap. Facebook visual Story. Theme: wonder. Hold for prose and image.

I came out underneath the log and thought I had reached the outside. A beetle walked below my caps. There was more of it down there.

Visible: several blue Mycena interrupta caps and pale stems attached around the lower side of dark wet wood. No beetle appears. Fiction: emergence as remembered experience, the passing beetle and Ludo's spatial realization. Several caps do not establish one genetic individual. The wood remains his substrate; he never walks or changes position as a mushroom.

Hold: a scale idea is doing the work of an event, and the final pronoun makes the payoff less clear. The image has attractive blue forms but uneven focus and substantial dark clutter; its original is only 1,000 by 750. It is not a strong Story pairing. The earlier guest misunderstanding and distributed-body Medium article already cover nearby territory. Preserve the source as a rejected candidate, not a catalog addition.

## 06. A useful answer

Tully Tumble. Instagram visual Story. Theme: social. Hold for prose.

The youngster asked whether I ever get frightened. I had a mouthful of grass. Chewed it very thoroughly while he waited.

Visible: one rock hyrax looks toward the camera while holding green stems at its open mouth. The youngster is absent. Fiction: the question, deliberate delay and remembered listener. Vegetation feeding is species-supported; the photograph does not document a conversation or fear.

Hold: the evasion is recognizable but ordinary, and nearly all the relationship lives outside the image. Tully already worries about his standing as a lookout. This gives him another evasive admission without a changed choice. Do not replace his accepted first-watch or branch scenes with it. The source is well resolved but the cluttered green setting needs a stronger reason to become a Story.

## 07. Forward together

Nessa Whisker. Facebook visual Story. Theme: play. Hold for image quality.

When we were cubs, I bit his fur to stop him going. He kept walking. I had to go too, which was not how I had meant to win.

Visible: two Arctic fox cubs on bare ground; one has its mouth at the other's shoulder or back while that animal faces forward. Fiction: Nessa's memory, their disagreement, the continued walk and the motive. The photograph does not identify sexes or siblings. No injury or distressed prey is invented.

Hold: the bodily reversal has potential, but the original photograph is a grainy 405 by 300 black-and-white image. It fails the requested image standard. Do not enlarge it and call it gorgeous, or bind it provisionally. Any future replacement must show the same non-injurious play clearly and preserve the explicit childhood memory so it does not disrupt Nessa's adult molt chronology.

## 08. The other way down

Fern Longnose. Instagram visual Story. Theme: movement. Hold for scene, prose and species certainty.

I climbed up to see where everyone was going. Found a branch that fitted my ribs. They will have to go somewhere visible from here.

Visible: a coati resting over a branch, with its face, chest and one set of claws in view; enclosure mesh is visible. Fiction: her climb, social purpose, comfort and refusal to move. Source title and description say only coati. A search match is not sufficient species verification for Fern's white-nosed-coati identity.

Hold: the captive portrait does not offer a distinctive enough action; the scene also approaches Fern's newly illustrated sleeping-fork episode and her spent habit of letting the group leave. Do not admit this image merely because the face appears compatible. The full species identity would need confirmation if the source were reconsidered for any use.
