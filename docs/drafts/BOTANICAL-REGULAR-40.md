## botanical40-01 Not quite finished

lionel-fringe; Coprinus comatus; facebook regular post.

The little white cap beside me is still keeping itself together. I was like that very recently.

Then I grew taller and lifted my rim. The edge turned black. A bead hung where I had been expecting more neat white fringe.

I thought I could finish opening before anything came off.

Now there is a black string of me hanging toward the grass. The younger cap has barely begun to lift.

I would like to appear reassuring. It is difficult to look experienced while visibly becoming a drip.

Visible evidence: A mature open cap with a black rim, a long black strand descending from its margin, and a younger white cylindrical cap in front. The strand and age contrast remain legible in the published hires file.

Fiction boundary: Remembered opening sequence, expectation and desire to reassure are fiction. The younger cap is an anonymous neighbor, not proven offspring or the same mycelium. The body is maturing and deliquescing, not recovering from injury.

## botanical40-02 A little overdressed

sylvia-bead; Rhodotus palmatus; instagram regular post.

I was pleased when the amber beads appeared on my stem. They went beautifully with the pink cap.

Then more came. Several ran together, and the lowest began to stretch.

For a while I tried to think of myself as richly decorated. There is now a large drop preparing to fall off the bottom of me.

I would have preferred to remain ornamental for a little longer.

Visible evidence: Pink reticulated cap, pale stem with many rounded amber drops and a larger pendant drop low on the stem. The source specifically identifies guttation.

Fiction boundary: Earlier small beads, merging drops and preference for an ornamental appearance are fiction grounded in the visible secreting structure. No distress, rainstorm or insect visitor is invented.
