## 01. Sylvie Stilt: When we came down

candidate. instagram post.

Every time she landed, I wanted to be in the air.

She bowed. I jumped. Came down while she was rising, and sprang up again before my legs had straightened. The grass brushed my toes. Then my feet hit a hollow and I had to take two quick steps to keep under my wings.

She landed beside me. We stood breathing, with our wings hanging loose.

I lowered my head. She lowered hers. This time we both jumped. I could see her feet beside mine, clear of the grass.

## 02. Tobias Tumble: A little farther up

candidate. facebook post.

I came out of the river with a bit of weed caught in the fur under my leg. Rolled onto my back, curled around it and started pulling it loose with my teeth.

The whole bank began moving uphill.

Straightened out. Dug my feet into the snow. Stopped with my tail hanging over the edge and the weed still attached.

Climbed back up and lay across the slope this time. Got the weed free. Worked through the fur around it, then the other side, without sliding anywhere.

When I was finished, I turned downhill and tucked my feet in.

Visible scene: A river otter lies on its back on sloping snow above a rocky edge, with limbs tucked near its body and its long tapering tail extended.

Weed, accidental slide, braking, climb, reorientation and deliberate final slide are fiction. No attached weed is visible. No injury, dangerous fall, drowning risk or claim that the animal confuses its limbs. The moving-bank sentence is subjective motion, resolved by the next actions.
