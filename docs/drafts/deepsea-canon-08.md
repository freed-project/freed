### Biological constraints

- Claude's source specifically identifies the anemone as Stylobates aeneus and says it creates a growing proteinaceous covering. Do not substitute the biology of any generic anemone riding a shell.
- Rue's source says modified fins support movement and the branched facial whiskers help sense food. The scene uses that documented physical method.
- [Glass sponges filter bacteria and plankton from water and have silica skeletons](https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/ocean-fact/glass-sponges/). Bertram's thought experiment is fictional, not a claim that a sponge has a brain. Celia's exact family/genus remains unresolved beyond the source wording.
- Dulcie's source explicitly says a sudden downcurrent took this individual out of the attempted collection. The interrupted conversation is fiction.
- [Hymenaster can project mucus when agitated](https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/multimedia/daily-image-media-20210213/). The draft extrapolates a fictional response, not a photographed interaction. Her pictured specimen was collected; do not invent a release or identify it as a described species.
- Gladys is a solitary hydroid, not a colony of interchangeable people. Source diameter exceeds a meter.
- Ned is identified only to Holasteroida, not a guessed rare species.
- Pip's source describes an undescribed stalked crinoid seen in high densities. The fictional synchronized dance is not a documented ritual.
- Sol's source describes an unusual chrysogorgiid with traits of two genera. Do not make up a new scientific name.
