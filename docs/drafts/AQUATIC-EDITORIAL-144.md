## Tavi Tilt: She had heard that part

She turned the eggs over while I was telling her about the shell. I stopped.

One eye came round to me. The other stayed on the eggs.

I said it could wait.

She asked whether it had broken on the first strike or the second.

I came closer and showed her the pieces.

## Image and prose decisions

One peacock mantis shrimp holds a dense pink-orange egg mass at a rock burrow entrance. Eyes are sharp and oriented differently. Upper eye is more tilted than foreground eye. No male or shell fragments appear.

The apparent distraction becomes evidence that she has followed him. He returns to the conversation by showing the pieces. This revisits independent eyes with a useful consequence, unlike Both unfortunately where his own eye follows the wrong individual. Main review risk: shell pieces and narrator are offscreen. The visually central brooding companion is genuinely present, but reject if the unseen shell supplies too much of the scene. No demand to admit simply because the image is strong.

## Canon and fiction

Visible brooder is a female companion, never male Tavi. Tavi narrates from outside the frame. Eggs turning, speech, specific eye targets, cracked shell, approach and attention are fiction. No fatherhood, male brooding, feeding of mother or shared burrow is asserted. Eye pose does not scientifically demonstrate divided social attention.

Append after Within reach, preserving all ten original episodes, male Tavi, avatar and Lembeh Strait home. Companion may be the established female but identity and continuity are fictional. Source is Bali, an illustrative compatible taxon and substrate, not a documented journey from Lembeh.

University of Michigan ADW species account describes female egg masses carried on front thoracic appendages, with cleaning and aeration. Research paper The independence of eye movements in a stomatopod crustacean is task dependent studies O. scyllarus and supports separately oriented mobile eyes, without claiming literal listening or unrestricted independent visual cognition.

[Female brooding biology](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Odontodactylus_scyllarus/); [primary eye-movement experiment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5399772/).
