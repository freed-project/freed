# Visual land Stories 03

Draft-only replacement proposals. No runtime entries changed or admitted. Four candidates and two holds, after inspecting six exact new photographs. All proposed replacements are currently image-less slots in `packages/shared/src/sample-character-arcs-land.ts`. Episode numbers below are one-based and preserve the existing stable position. Set `contentType: "story"` only after independent approval.

## 01. A smaller breath

Replace Tully Tumble (`tully-tumble`) episode 4, **A better mouthful**. Instagram Story, movement. Subject: rock hyrax.

I stopped halfway along to let the branch finish creaking. It stopped when I breathed in. I have been taking very small breaths.

Visible: a round-bodied hyrax crouches lengthwise on a slender descending branch, with its feet gripping underneath. Fiction: creaking, breathing, the inferred connection between them. The joke belongs to Tully's body in this precarious-looking position, not an actual claim that hyrax breathing changes branch load. The old episode's disappointing high shoot is removed. No later episode explicitly depends on that shoot. Do not present this as evidence that the animal was distressed during photography.

Mechanism: a sensible pause turns into negotiating with one's own breathing. Candidate; reject if the imagined creak feels too detached from the visible posture.

## 02. His first watch

Replace Tully Tumble episode 10, **The other end of the ledge**. Facebook Story, cooperation. Subject: rock hyrax.

His first turn on watch. I stood behind him so I could see over his head. He leaned back until his ears touched my chin. We watched like that. There was less horizon, but he stayed.

Visible: three hyraxes together on a rocky slope. A smaller animal stands immediately in front of an adult, nearly against its chest. Another adult faces left. Fiction: the first watch, the youngster's deliberate lean, and the narrator's decision to accommodate him. Do not infer parentage from the caption or assign a new child to Tully. This can be the existing unnamed youngster from episodes 3 and 7. It advances that relationship from reluctantly sharing warmth to making space for confidence, without another denial of affection.

Mechanism: a useful task is imperfectly performed because the learner needs bodily reassurance. Candidate, intentionally tender rather than a forced joke. Preserve the false-alarm and eagle callback in episodes 2 and 8 untouched.

## 03. A little farther over

Replace Wren Boulder (`wren-boulder`) episode 6, **A dry little island**. Instagram Story, social. Subject: klipspringer.

I asked him to stand a little farther over. He moved one hoof. I moved two. We have exchanged nearly the whole rock without either of us leaving it.

Visible: close framing of two klipspringers' legs and small hooves on adjacent parts of a rounded rock. No heads are shown; do not pretend the photograph identifies either animal's sex. Fiction: Wren and her existing unnamed companion, the request, and the step sequence. Preserve their watch-sharing and the growing-shoot sequence elsewhere. This is spatial negotiation in a close pair, not a new mating scene or general statement about all couples.

Mechanism: many tiny accommodations accomplish a surprisingly large rearrangement. Candidate. The hoof detail is the subject, so do not crop it to a generic animal portrait.

## 04. The flowering one

Replace Kit Snowshoe (`kit-snowshoe`) episode 3, **Drying weather**. Facebook Story, wonder. Subject: American pika.

One stem tickled my nose all the way home. I could have dropped it. It was the only one still flowering.

Visible: pika carrying a flowering plant across green alpine vegetation beside rocks. A fine upright flowering stem reaches beside its nose. Source identifies the plant as spotted saxifrage. Fiction: tickling, the journey home, and Kit's preference for that particular stem. There are other flowers in the scene; “the only one” means the carried stems, not the whole meadow. If this distinction is not clear enough in the rendered Story, change to “I liked the flowers on that one” or hold, rather than claiming the image depicts the meadow's last flower.

Mechanism: an anxious gatherer tolerates a small nuisance for beauty rather than storage efficiency. Candidate with a lower-confidence landing: quiet affection for a plant must feel earned by the close image, not like another deliberately slight observation. This occupies the existing summer position before snow episodes 7 to 9. It does not undo the haypile or repeat the oversized-doorway joke in episode 1.

## 05. The last bit of me

Potential replacement for Fern Longnose (`fern-longnose`) episode 2, **Last again**. Facebook Story, movement. Subject: white-nosed coati. **Hold, do not admit.**

I sat down to wait for the others. My tail kept going. For once, a part of me arrived somewhere first.

Visible: seated coati on stony earth, long tail extended behind. Fiction: waiting, the tail's previous motion, its supposed arrival. Rejected by this author: reverses an existing habit only verbally; the static tail does not supply a convincing event. The busy ground and distant framing are less beautiful than the other photographs. Do not use a mediocre image to fill a quota.

## 06. A very large interruption

Potential replacement for Fern Longnose episode 6, **Borrowed vigilance**. Instagram Story, social. Subject: white-nosed coati. **Hold, do not admit.**

I opened my mouth to complain. A yawn came out first, much larger than the complaint. By the time it finished, I wanted to lie down with whoever I had been cross with.

Visible: same coati and ground as 05, photographed with mouth wide open and tongue visible. Source explicitly calls it yawning. Fiction: complaint and reconciliation. Rejected by this author: unseen dispute carries too much of the payoff; repeats the threat-to-body-interruption device already used by Penny and Nib. The two coati frames are also too similar to admit together. Existing episode remains provisional, not replaced by this weaker text.

## Biology and continuity limits

[Smithsonian's rock hyrax account](https://www.nationalzoo.si.edu/animals/rock-hyrax) was freshly read: vegetation feeding, climbing ability, colonies, and young mobile soon after birth support Tully's physical and social setting. It does not document this fictional first watch. Photos 01 and 02 were made in Damaraland, not Tully's canonical Matobo home; they illustrate fictional scenes without moving his home or claiming a photographic sighting of him.

[San Diego Zoo's klipspringer account](https://animals.sandiegozoo.org/animals/klipspringer) was freshly read: small hooves, rock habitat, watch-sharing pairs. Photo 03's Serengeti setting agrees with Wren's existing home. Do not overstate suction mechanics or infer photographed sex from legs.

Photo 04's source explicitly identifies haying, spotted saxifrage, and Moraine Lake. The attempted NPS pika biology page failed to load, so this draft does not claim fresh NPS verification. No specialized claim beyond the photographed carrying action enters the proposed text. Retain Kit's fictional Rocky Mountain home and seasonal order rather than relocating the character to the photo locality.

The coati proposals are holds. No source-location or individual-sex claim has been made for Fern from these frames. All six use first-person fictional narration; none invent a real-world philosophical quotation or place philosophy attribution on a Story channel.
