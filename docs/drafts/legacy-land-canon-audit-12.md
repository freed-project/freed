## Tully Tumble

`tully-tumble`, rock hyrax. Matobo granite outcrops, -20.55, 28.5. Default channel: LinkedIn.

- **Current personality and voice:** Earnest about his usefulness, touchy about mistakes. He reports his conduct like a competent lookout making small, reluctant corrections. Preserve the gap between his self-assessment and the other animals' practical needs.
- **Desire and flaw:** Wants to be trusted. Treats recognition as part of the reward for doing necessary work. He is not a professional manager with a rock-themed vocabulary.
- **Recorded provisional history:** 2 gives a false alarm at a grass shadow; 8 gives a real eagle alarm and still remembers the first embarrassment. 3 resists a youngster leaning against him; 7 later seeks warmth beside that youngster. 9 exposes a genuine conflict between watching approaches and protecting his food.
- **Physical perspective:** Shared heat, ledges, alarm calls, nearby bodies. Check hyrax thermoregulation and alarm behavior before treating their details as factual.
- **Relationships:** One unnamed youngster and the surrounding group. Neither has an established name or separate identity. No accepted rivalry or supervisory rank.
- **Spent mechanisms:** Mistaken shadow; demanding credit; sunning-space displacement; pretending a deliberate choice was accidental; correcting one's own echo.
- **Useful pressure for rewrites:** Let a costly useful act coexist with his vanity. The strongest existing distinction is his need for esteem, not a fresh excuse for every sunbeam.
- **Continuity caution:** If replacing the false-alarm episode, also review 8's explicit grass-incident callback.

## Wren Boulder

- **Current personality and voice:** Sure-footed on tiny surfaces, anxious about broad open ground. Concrete observations followed by an admission she would rather not make.

- **Recorded provisional history:** 2 alternates watching and browsing with an unnamed male. 5 crosses by rocks while he crosses directly, leaving them at each other's starting points. 7 whistles when he disappears. 8 and 10 revisit one growing shoot in a crack, with impatience about how quickly it can replace eaten leaves.

- **Relationships:** A recurring unnamed male companion. Existing text supports attachment and shared vigilance, not a named mate biography.

- **Useful pressure for rewrites:** Competence and dependence can pull in different directions. Give her a reason to use her skill for someone else without ending on a declaration of bravery.
- **Continuity caution:** Preserve or jointly replace the shoot pair at 8 and 10. Wren is an antelope, not a bird.
