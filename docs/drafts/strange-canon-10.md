## Peri Bracken

Retain the existing optimistic peacock butterfly, Aglais io, and her Oxford meadow location. Pip remains a remembered larval companion, not an identified adult in a photograph. These proposals add two earlier larval memories and two adult experiences. The lead should place the memories as memories rather than implying that Peri becomes a larva again after her existing adult entries.

- 01: Her affection for Pip is sincere and physically awkward. The photograph shows one larva, so contact is a fictional memory, not a claim that two animals are pictured.
- 02: Bodily change makes her contradictory and irritable. She wants company and solitude in quick succession. Transformation does not supply better manners. Do not promote this imagined mood or memory into a biological finding.
- 03: Her enjoyment exceeds the minimum needed to resume flight. Keep the pleasure without attaching a moral about productivity.
- 04: A fly is actually visible beside the butterfly. She becomes curious about its experience, then loses track of her own sip. The fly's species and sensory organs remain unspecified.

Protect the existing crowded-nettle and adult loneliness stories. These additions should not all appear consecutively: the two larval memories belong to the same phase but have different social stakes.

## Ludo Bluecap

Retain Mycena interrupta, damp Tasmanian forest, and his slightly fussy delight in being small. He cannot walk, repair his cap, or move furniture. Several visible caps do not prove one genetic individual. His first-person attachment to them is fictional narration.

- 05: An actual damaged cap supplies the leak. The guest is unpictured fiction; it solves the inconvenience by shifting position. Avoid expanding this into another host-versus-neighbor quarrel.
- 06: The old log remembers when a small bird could bend it. Ludo initially takes its remembered scale literally. The final wren makes the old tree's earlier body comprehensible. Tree memory, conversation, and the visitor are explicitly animistic fiction. No bird is pictured, and no observed bird species is claimed. The generic narrator word wren must not become a scientific identification in metadata.
- 07: Immobility suits a particular pleasure. Do not turn this into universal fungal enlightenment.
- 08: A flexible stem carries the end of a gust a little beyond the gust itself. This is a short sensory candidate, not a long plot. The lead may reject it if the combined photo and brevity do not earn a place.

The fungal habitat baseline remains the Australian National Botanic Gardens account: https://www.anbg.gov.au/fungi/mycogeography-distant.html. It supports the organism's geography and wood-associated life, not fictional sensations or conversations.

No claim is made that the pictures show the same organism aging. The established taxonomic uncertainty remains; do not silently identify every photograph as one narrowly defined species.
