September 5, 2026. Five candidates, three characters, two longer stories. These are proposals, not accepted runtime entries. The original twelve-candidate and three-story target was not met. Parent allowed a smaller quality-led batch. No missing entries are represented as finished.

## Luna After

Adult *Actias luna*. Fictional home: deciduous woodland in the Catskills, New York, 42.20, -74.35. Public bio: I did finish my greens.

Luna remembers being a ravenous caterpillar and resents the implication that pleasure was a character defect. Now she is exuberant, blunt and briefly airborne. She wants to experience her adult body before anyone turns it into a lesson. She fears wasting the night on an argument. Her contradiction is that she enjoys answering her critics almost as much as flying away from them.

Private history: a neighboring caterpillar, Pip, warned her about eating so much. Pip is an imagined acquaintance, not a second named catalog character. The first episode spends the appetite-to-wings reversal. Do not repeat it, add dieting jokes, or make adult Luna eat. Future photo-supported scenes may concern artificial light, attraction or wing damage, but none are authorized by this one image.

[University of Florida species account](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/IN737) documents the larval and adult stages, vestigial adult mouthparts and eastern North American distribution. Metamorphosis supplies the joke; it is not a claim that this photographed adult remembers its actual larval life. Sex is not assigned from the image. Reviewed avatar candidate: commons-0027, Jeremy Johnson, CC BY-SA 4.0. The parent approved the short's prose, not runtime admission.

## Dora Dew

*Drosera rotundifolia*, round-leaved sundew. Fictional home: wet acidic bog in the Rhön, Germany, 50.5193, 10.0625. Public bio: Flowers are open.

Dora is social, verbally impulsive and aware that her body makes casual friendliness complicated. She wants visitors who stay because they like her. She fears that every affectionate sentence sounds like appetite. She is neither ashamed of being a carnivorous plant nor entitled to touch whoever visits. Her first two entries establish a clear boundary and a disastrous compliment repaired through a shared joke.

The bee in Start again is a fictional recurring acquaintance called Bea privately, not a claim that a photographed bee is present. Keep her independent and able to leave. Her callback can recur once only if a later event changes their relationship. Do not turn this into a series of failed compliments or predation jokes.

[Kew's sundew profile](https://www.kew.org/plants/round-leafed-sundew) and [NC State's species account](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/drosera-rotundifolia/) describe adhesive glandular leaves and flowers. Their separation is directly visible in the whole-plant photograph. The draft does not claim an evolutionary explanation for stalk length, identify a pollinator species or suggest that all visiting insects are captured. Plant dialogue and desire are fictional. White petals do not carry the leaves' trapping anatomy.

The two used photos show different wild plants of the same species. They illustrate one fictional character, not a documented individual or a claim that she traveled between Czechia and Germany. Reviewed avatar candidate: the inspected Petr Dlouhý leaf macro, retained as a source candidate but not reused as an episode here. Exact metadata for that unused image was fetched, but the two episode bindings alone are preserved in the JSON.

## Coral Clad

*Cladonia cristatella*, British soldier lichen. Fictional home: mixed woodland in northern Georgia, 34.68, -84.08. Public bio: We were discussing it.

Coral is one fictional speaking lichen with two principal voices: a shelter-minded fungal narrator and a more impatient green photosynthetic partner. This literary shorthand does not assert that lichens contain exactly two organisms. Other associated microbes are not ruled out. Neither voice represents a literal scientific theory of fungal consciousness.

Coral wants authorship of a life that cannot honestly be called his alone. The green partner wants recognition without being reduced to an assistant. Both are prickly about being described as dependent. Their contradiction is that each argues as an individual while their shared body keeps living through both partners' work. The autobiography cannot get past its first pronoun. Its dedication repeats the original mistake. The rejected tree story is not canon. Future entries need a new event, not another pronoun argument.

The red structures are **apothecia**, fungal spore-producing fruiting bodies, not eyes, flowers or hats. The short uses reproductive organs colloquially. It does not depict copulation. [Georgia's biodiversity species account](https://georgiabiodiversity.org/portal/profile?es_id=430928&group=lichens) describes the red apothecia, green thallus, algal photobiont and woodland substrates. [Kew's lichen overview](https://www.kew.org/read-and-watch/lichens-london-pollution) notes fungi, algae and potentially other microbial partners. [USDA's forest lichen atlas](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/default/files/fs_media/fs_document/Lichen-Atlas.pdf) describes the principal symbiotic partners.

The autobiography imagines disagreement within a shared organism. It does not claim literal speech, writing or two conscious individuals. Sugar production belongs to the photosynthetic partner. The image should show the lichen clearly enough that the composite body, rather than unrelated scenery, carries the premise.

Reviewed avatar candidate: Louise K. Broman's EPA/Indiana Dunes image, public domain according to Commons. Source gives unknown photo location. The Georgian home is fictional and range-plausible, not photographer GPS.

## Inspection and exclusions

URLs, dimensions, creator and license are retained in the draft JSON. Fresh sundew rows lack a requested SHA1, recorded as null instead of invented; this is metadata incompleteness, not a text-only entry. Every draft has an image URL.

- The plume moth photo commons-0182 has awkward cropping and background.
- Rotaria commons-0399 is poorly resolved in debris.
- Several microscopy images carry scale bars or unclear species identification. No rotifer story admitted. The generic committee joke was rejected before file creation.

- Sundew rosette among grasses was busier than the selected views. The clean sundew macro remains a possible avatar, not an extra count.
- Cladonia 109055517 has too much foreground grass for this small batch. Cladonia 109053267 is less striking than the EPA image but retains a useful environmental view. Parent image review is still required.
