## 38-01 Loose ends

A stick lodged between my arms. Then another. I blamed the wind until the sticks began crossing one another in a rather deliberate way.

By the time the nest was finished, there was more dead wood between those arms than I had ever intended to hold. Loose ends stuck out on every side.

The wind came back.

I had been waiting for it to clear the mess away. Now I wanted each badly placed twig to stay exactly where it was.

## 38-02 Entirely my own

The mesquite put a branch between my arms when there was still plenty of room. We both kept growing.

Now her leaves are among my spines, and a woody twig lies across the ribs I had considered entirely my own. I have spent several seasons being offended by her poor sense of distance.

A nub of mine has come up on the other side of the branch. Her leaves are caught around that, too.

I was about to blame her for this arrangement. Then I reached the part where I had grown around her.

Image finding: Mesquite leaves and woody branches visibly intersect the cactus arms, ribs and nubs. No fused tissue or fresh injury is claimed.

Fiction boundary: The order and duration of intergrowth, territorial feelings and reassessment are fiction. Actual visible interleaving supports the conflict; no voluntary reaching or rapid growth is claimed.
