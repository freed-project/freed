# Nova canon review 08

Six existing image/prose pairs reviewed: zero keeps, six holds. Two new Cassiopeia A files are candidates for independent lead review. No runtime files changed. Nova 5 is protected verbatim.

## Evidence and chronology

Reviewed the editorial guide, full character canon, all seven literal Nova episodes and the base-07 exclusion. Every one of the six bound files and both proposed files was individually opened. Live Commons image metadata was fetched for all eight; all report public domain. Existing source SHA-1 values match live metadata. SHA-1 below identifies the original Commons asset, not a resized thumbnail. Temporary media and metadata remain outside Git at `/tmp/freed-nova-review-08/`.

The arc requires a massive-star core collapse: long fusion history, inward collapse, outward explosion, interaction with outside gas, dispersed iron, grains, destruction of grains. Kepler and SN 1006 are white-dwarf Type Ia events. CTB 1 has a pulsar and is compatible with core collapse. The galactic-center candidate is unconfirmed. Merely changing a name would not resolve these contradictions.

Cassiopeia A provides a coherent visual source for a massive-star remnant, explicitly classified as core collapse by [NASA Chandra](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/chandra/nasas-chandra-reveals-stars-inner-conflict-before-explosion/). Its observed remnant is only roughly 340 years old from Earth’s perspective, so the bio’s millennia and future journeys must describe the narrated life across time, not the age of every photograph. Do not silently equate Cas A with an older CTB 1 scene. The main editor should establish whether Nova uses one named object or compatible illustrative analogues.

## Replacement A: iron remains visible

Recommended for protected Nova 5 with its exact existing title and body. Also suitable as an avatar if the lead selects this visual identity. Do not use it simultaneously as another repeated episode image.

I opened the 560 × 560 original: a mottled pink, green and blue rounded shell against dark space, brighter tangled ridges at left and top. It has no labels or graphic border. Detail is modest but the complete shape reads clearly. [The primary Chandra source](https://chandra.si.edu/photo/1999/casajph/) identifies iron-enriched material on the left outer edge. The colors encode X-ray energy, not a literal red-iron photograph. This is the physical anchor the protected passage needs. Its future world and blood remain explicitly imagined future, not a claim that Cas A already seeded Earth.

## Replacement B: Nova 1 and avatar option

I opened the 576 × 576 original: bright blue fractured shell against black, with a protruding jet at upper left and a lacy, darker interior. No labels, logo or border. Strong avatar silhouette and sharper visible structure than A. [Chandra’s matching source](https://chandra.si.edu/photo/2004/casa/more.html) identifies the shock ring and jet. It shows the aftermath, not the intact star.

Nova 1’s current literal future-tense ending remains HOLD against either remnant file:

> I spent millions of years making heavier elements. The iron stayed in my center, where no eye could reach it. You will see that part of me later. I will have to come apart first.

An honest framing option, offered as a draft rather than an admitted replacement:

Title: The long before

> I spent millions of years making heavier elements. This is what is left of the furnace.

This places the visible remnant in the narrator’s present and treats stellar burning as memory. It changes the opening from a pre-explosion scene to retrospective narration, so the lead must review its place before the collapse and explosion flashbacks. If the original chronology and exact wording must remain, keep slot 1 unbound until a legitimate progenitor scene is found. Neither image depicts that scene.
