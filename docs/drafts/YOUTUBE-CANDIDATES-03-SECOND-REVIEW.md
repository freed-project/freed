## Dorian: footage and taxonomy recovered

The working primary route is [NOAA's archived dive 6 photolog](https://archive.oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/okeanos/explorations/ex1606/logs/photolog/dive6.html). Its source HTML directly supplies:

- [Native SD footage](https://archive.oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/okeanos/explorations/ex1606/dailyupdates/media/video/dive6/EX1606_VID_20160805_DIVE06_SEA_TOAD_Stream_640x360.mp4).
- [Native HD footage](https://archive.oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/okeanos/explorations/ex1606/dailyupdates/media/video/dive6/EX1606_VID_20160805_DIVE06_SEA_TOAD_Stream_1920x1080.mp4).
- [Captions](https://archive.oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/okeanos/explorations/ex1606/dailyupdates/media/video/dive6/dive6_captions.vtt).
- [Daily update and image source](https://archive.oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/okeanos/explorations/ex1606/dailyupdates/aug6.html).

I sampled native SD footage at ten-second intervals with ffmpeg and opened `/tmp/freed-toad-second-review.png`. Around 10 to 30 seconds the fish is rounded and resting beside a rock. By approximately 50 to 70 seconds its lower body is less distended and it changes support posture. Later samples show it swimming over the substrate, then landing. The image sequence establishes the change in body shape and subsequent movement. It is not a physiological measurement, uninterrupted video review, or an audio review.

The exact [daily-update still](https://archive.oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/okeanos/explorations/ex1606/dailyupdates/media/aug6-1-hires.jpg) is independently opened at `/tmp/freed-toad-second-still.jpg`. It shows the same pink, prickly fish with its rounded body, downturned mouth, splayed pectoral fins, and the same dark-rock/pale-sediment setting. The rounded body carries the imposing-presence premise; the video supplies its reversal.

Proposed media evidence:

- Dimensions: 1920 × 1080.
- SHA1: `7f32a429cae0d5d339cc3a66014791645b8c2ee5`.
- Credit: NOAA Office of Ocean Exploration and Research, Deepwater Wonders of Wake.
- Source date/location: August 6, 2016, unnamed guyot west of Wake Island. Do not turn this into a new home or a physical trip from Dorian's Musicians Seamounts home.
- Alt text: Pink sea toad rests with a rounded body and spread fins on pale sediment beside dark rocks.
- Reuse basis: NOAA-only expedition credit, subject to the [NOAA reuse FAQ](https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/about/faq/#permission); preserve the credit. No YouTube stream was downloaded or rehosted.

The source filename contains August 5, while the page states August 6. Preserve the exact asset URL and the page's stated observation date without silently reconciling that discrepancy.

[NOAA's later taxonomy article](https://archive.oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/okeanos/explorations/ex1708/logs/sept12/welcome.html) explicitly identifies a matching August 6, 2016 Wake encounter photograph as **Chaunacops coloratus**, at 2,239 meters. I independently opened its [identified image](https://archive.oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/okeanos/explorations/ex1708/logs/sept12/media/img4-hires.jpg), review file `/tmp/freed-toad-taxonomy-still.jpg`, SHA1 `e6829b26c60d34ce7c08b733eaaacb767bb09cb5`. It shows the compatible pink prickly form, broad sensory pits, fin support and rocky setting. This resolves the genus concern sufficiently for Dorian's existing genus-level identity. It does not make all Dorian pictures one species or one tracked animal.

The taxonomy article says some related fishes can expel water through narrow gill openings. It does not specifically document the mechanism of this filmed volume change. The original “most of my presence was water” should therefore remain held. Proposed replacement for lead prose review:

> I sat there being an imposing presence until I needed to swim.

This retains Dorian's terse irritation and makes the physical reversal carry the joke. The final clause is fictional motive. It avoids claiming a measured duration, water mechanism or real observer dialogue. The still and movement pass this independent scene-fit review; final prose and canon admission remain with the lead.
