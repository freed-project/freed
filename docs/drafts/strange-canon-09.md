# Orla and Nib: replacement proposals

Twelve proposals for existing null-image episodes. The lead approved six replacements, including all three longer stories. They are integrated in the same runtime slots with six explicit image bindings. No new characters or additions to the corpus total. Exact old title, old body, one-based episode number and expected former null binding remain in `strange-image-led-09.json` as replacement evidence. Aggregate media wiring and publication are not claimed.

Accepted draft IDs: 01, 03, 04, 05, 07 and 10. These replace Orla 2, 4, 6 and 7, plus Nib 2 and 5. The six-photo metadata file is `packages/shared/src/sample-corpus-legacy-land-nine-media.json`. The existing land tuple helper now accepts an optional platform so the three stories use RSS without changing their character or episode position.

Unadmitted: 02, 06, 08 and 09 are weak; 11 uses an artificial setup; 12 does not show the claimed mouthful or held bamboo. Entry 12's scene description is corrected in JSON. Its photo is a portrait beside bamboo, not an eating photograph. Do not carry those six proposals into future canon as accepted events.

## Orla Reach

Existing identity, bio and Tasmanian location are retained: short-beaked echidna; dry woodland, -42.5, 147.1. No new slug or relocation is proposed.

Her established nose-led appetite and practical digging remain in the retained episodes. These candidates broaden her experience beyond logs: bodily intimacy, being investigated, motherhood, a dream, dangerous manufactured ground and a tactile encounter whose other participant stays unidentified.

She is blunt rather than invulnerable. In the road story she wants diggable ground, not an abstract lesson about humans. Her first maternal memory adds a child who remains unnamed and whose later life is not invented. The photograph illustrates the mother's mature spines; it does not show a puggle or prove this individual has reproduced.

The [Australian Museum species account](https://australian.museum/learn/animals/mammals/short-beaked-echidna/) supports the Tasmanian distribution, digging and a puggle leaving the pouch as spines develop. [San Diego Zoo's account](https://animals.sandiegozoo.org/animals/echidna) supports adult toothlessness, a soft face and defensive digging. Dreaming of teeth and human-like reflections are fiction, not claims about recorded dream content. No nipples, expelled quills, venom or toothed adult is introduced. The mother's invented bedtime and tenderness must not be treated as a documented husbandry schedule.

Image limits are important here. The six images show different animals, including mainland photographs and two explicitly Tasmanian views. No seasonal transformation or subspecies equivalence is claimed. The lead should judge whether visible fur differences are acceptable for a fictional Tasmanian identity. Entry 05 actually depicts a road crossing; its photographer reports slowing after mutual alarm. The source does not depict a collision. Entry 02 does not show a fly. Entry 06's small gap is real, but the unseen contact is fiction. The puggle story uses the visible mature body as the reason for a remembered developmental event, not as a photograph of that event.

## Nib Willow

Existing identity, bio and Singalila home remain: red panda; 27.09, 88.03. The bad sleeping fork and later missing lump remain canonical dependencies, not casualties of the rewrite.

Nib still wants comfort and particular leaves. His longer childhood story makes the desire for help more specific: after learning to hold bamboo, he loosens his grip to keep his mother's hand underneath. This adds a remembered mother, not a current family group or a claim that adults live in a troop. He can be mildly rude, sleepy halfway through a threat and suspicious of a human-made ladder without becoming a generic commentator on human society.

The [Red Panda Network facts](https://redpandanetwork.org/post/15-Fantastic-Facts-about-Red-Pandas) support climbing and grasping anatomy; [San Diego Zoo](https://animals.sandiegozoo.org/animals/red-panda) supports the bamboo-centered life. The teaching scene is a fictional memory, not a documented red-panda teaching protocol. No prehensile tail is claimed. Photo metadata naming *Ailurus fulgens* is retained without pretending to establish genetic species assignment for every captive photograph.

Image limits: the wild headfirst image is explicitly from Singalila. Several others are zoo photographs. Entry 11 acknowledges the visible rope-and-stick structure instead of disguising it as a natural branch. It may still be a poor fit for the wild character and is a lead-review decision. The mouth-open image cannot establish whether the animal was growling or yawning, so that transition remains a fictional scene. The adult bamboo grip supports the remembered childhood skill, not a pictured mother. Two final bamboo views have similar composition and may need rejection for visual repetition.

## Inspection and admission boundary

All twelve selected local files were opened individually. Their exact source URLs, original dimensions, creators, licenses, Commons SHA1 values and downloaded-file SHA256 hashes are retained in JSON. Images remain outside the repository at `/tmp/freed-strange09-review.A2onVH/`. Entry order differs from filename order; use each entry's `localPath` rather than guessing from its ID.

Rejected during source selection: a tiny 320-pixel panda image, and an irrelevant swimming query that returned non-echidnas. No claimed swimming photograph was invented. The selected road image is useful because of its actual scene, not because the animal fills the frame.

The three longer stories are 03, 05 and 07, all approved after independent lead image review. The approved histories add Orla's maternal memory, toothless dream, physical joke and road crossing, plus Nib's remembered lesson and interrupted growl. Six other entries remain rejected or held, not canon. No philosopher names or quotations appear. No prey's suffering supplies a punchline.
